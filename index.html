      📚 Quick Guides

      How EMI is Actually Calculated

      Your EMI isn't just "loan ÷ months" — banks use a reducing-balance formula where interest is charged only on the outstanding principal each month, not the original loan amount. The formula is EMI = P × r × (1+r)ⁿ / ((1+r)ⁿ−1), where P is principal, r is the monthly interest rate, and n is the number of months. In the early years of any loan, a larger share of your EMI goes toward interest rather than principal — which is exactly why prepaying early saves more than prepaying later. Try it yourself with our EMI Calculator or see the long-term effect with the Prepayment Savings tool.

      GST Inclusive vs Exclusive — Don't Mix Them Up

      A huge number of billing mistakes in small businesses come from confusing GST-inclusive and GST-exclusive pricing. If a product is priced at ₹1,000 inclusive of 18% GST, the actual base price isn't ₹1,000 minus 18% (₹820) — it's ₹1,000 ÷ 1.18 = ₹847.46, because the 18% is calculated on the base price, not the final price. Getting this wrong on invoices is one of the more common GST filing headaches. Our GST Calculator handles both directions correctly so you don't have to do the algebra by hand.

      What Actually Affects Your CIBIL-Based Loan Rate

      Lenders in India typically slot borrowers into rate bands based on CIBIL score: 750+ usually gets the best rates, 700–749 is still good, 650–699 means noticeably higher interest, and below 650 makes approval difficult regardless of income. But CIBIL isn't just about timely payments — your credit utilization ratio (how much of your available credit limit you're using) and the mix of secured vs unsecured loans both matter. Keeping utilization under 30% across your cards is one of the fastest ways to improve your score before applying. Check where you'd likely land with our Loan Eligibility Calculator.

      KML vs KMZ — and Why Your File Might Not Open

      KML is a plain XML-based file format for geographic data (used in Google Earth, GIS tools, and real estate boundary maps). KMZ is just a zipped version of the same thing, often bundling extra images or icons inside. If a "KML" file won't open in a viewer, it's frequently because it's actually a renamed KMZ — try renaming it to .zip and extracting it first to get the real .kml file inside. Our KML Viewer works directly with .kml files, right in your browser, with nothing uploaded.

      Old vs New Tax Regime — The One Number That Decides It

      The new tax regime offers lower slab rates but removes most deductions (80C, HRA exemption, home loan interest, etc.). The old regime keeps those deductions but taxes you at higher slab rates. As a rough rule of thumb: if your total eligible deductions exceed roughly ₹3.5–4 lakh annually, the old regime usually still wins; below that, the new regime tends to come out ahead. The honest answer is it depends entirely on your specific numbers, not a general rule — run both and compare directly with our Income Tax Calculator.

      Why Your Compressed Image Still Looks Sharp

      JPEG compression works by discarding image data that the human eye is less sensitive to — mostly fine color variation — rather than just shrinking resolution. That's why a JPEG at 70% quality can be 4-5x smaller than the original with almost no visible difference, while a PNG (which doesn't use this kind of "lossy" compression) stays large no matter how much you squeeze it. For photos, always compress as JPEG; for screenshots and graphics with sharp edges or text, PNG holds quality better. Try both with our Image Compressor.
